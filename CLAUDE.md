# Pocket Life (working name)

Luke's mobile "life in your pocket" game: SEED meets One Hour One Life. Built step by step with Claude.

## Design decisions (keep to these)
- Soft low-poly 3D like SEED, minimal UI like One Hour One Life. Luke is very particular about the look: don't restyle without asking.
- One person per player. When you die you start as a new person in the same world.
- Real-time 24-hour clock and real dates (the world runs on the player's real local time, day and night follow it).
- Age is chosen when making a person (18 to 90). Male or female, male is the default with short hair.
- Players are 13+. Deaths: hunger, thirst, tiredness, old age, animals, accidents. No PvP.
- Home storage can't be taken, but if a player is gone too long and upkeep lapses, it goes out to the world.
- Start-from-nothing world. Every item has a weight (stick 1, stone 1). Carry capacity 30.
- Berries are picked one at a time from berry bushes into your inventory, and eaten from there one at a time (2 hunger each). A full bush holds 5, and picked berries grow back one by one (a bare bush is full again after regrowHours, 4). One drink at the pond gives 10 thirst. These are playtest amounts; proper eating, drinking and resting tasks come later.
- Your person walks round rocks, bushes, logs, stumps and tree trunks.
- Controls (same on phone and computer): double-tap or double-click the ground to walk (the first tap leaves a faint ring, so a stray tap doesn't send you off). Tap or click a stick, stone, berry bush or the pond to open a small menu (Pick up Stick, Harvest Berries, Drink, Walk here); right-click or holding a finger down opens it too. A walk joins the end of the task queue, so it never interrupts anything. A small list above the bottom bar shows what your person is doing now (or "Waiting for something to do") and what's next, with an x on each task and Stop all. Escape, or the Menu button at the top right, opens the pause menu (Resume, Inventory, New person). On a computer, W A S D move the camera, Q and E turn it round your person, and R and F tilt it.
- While testing, settings.autoName fills in a name from testNames on the Make your person screen.
- Online (started in 0.0.6): one world for everyone. Both links join the same world (the live one): the test link is only for trying a new build in the real world before everyone gets it. Keep test builds' world layout the same as the release, or shared items and bushes won't line up. Other people show with a name tag and walk about; the Say button (or Enter) shows a speech bubble over your head. Sticks, stones and berries are shared: the server holds them, picking up or harvesting asks the server (if someone beat you to it, it comes back out of your inventory), and drops go through the server. Online, the world tops up one stick and one stone every respawnMinutes (10) while there are fewer on the ground than startInWorld: sticks under trees, stones by boulders (later this slows down once sticks come from trees and stones from rocks). The first player into a new world sends its starting layout (seed). Your person and inventory stay saved on your phone. The HUD shows a dot (green online, orange connecting, grey offline) and how many are online. The Menu has Graphics (Low, Medium, High, saved on the device) and Send feedback (stored on the server for the admin page). Chat safety: bad words in speech bubbles show as stars, and a bad name gets 'Pick a different name' (chatFilter in game-data.json; tools/worlds.py test-build copies it to server/words.json for the server). Tap another person for Mute (hides their bubbles, saved on the phone), Report (reason plus note, stored on the server with what they said lately) and Walk here. Names also can't contain admin, mod and similar (chatFilter.names). The server limits how fast each player can chat, pick up, harvest, drop, send feedback and report (LIMITS in server/src/index.js); too fast gets 'Slow down a little'. Each phone has a random id; others only see a scrambled tag of it. Decorative pebbles and flint are no longer drawn (still laid out, so nothing else moves) because they looked like stones you could pick up. Admin player: typing /admin and the code in the Say box (never said out loud) makes that phone the admin, remembered on the phone (/admin off forgets it). The code is the Cloudflare secret ADMIN_CODE that Luke set; it's never in the repo. The Menu then has Admin: maintenance on or off with a message (release-link players see Back soon; the test link and the admin still get in), message everyone (a banner), Feedback, reports, players (tabs: Feedback, Reports, Online, Banned), admin tag on or off (shows 'Name · Admin'), and Reset the world (tap twice; everyone rejoins and the first lays out the starting layout). Holding a finger on the ground gives Add <item> and Teleport here; tapping a person adds Mute for everyone, Kick, Ban (by phone) and Teleport to them; tapping an item adds Remove. Next: trading (both players accept), then needs and people saved on the server, and admin controls. No direct giving for now.
- Away from the game (planned for 0.0.7, needs people saved on the server first): your person stays in the world 24/7. For the first 24 hours away they keep themselves going: wander a little near where you left them, eat from their inventory, pick berries from nearby bushes, and walk to the pond to drink. If they still run out of food or water they can die (water is always there; add more berry bushes if too many die). After 24 hours away they lie down asleep on the ground (needs pause). After 7 days asleep they're gone and their inventory drops where they slept. Coming back shows a short "While you were away" note.
- Later: everything saved on the server, day planner, skills, more stats, regions and time zones, app store wrapper.

## How the code is laid out
- Files (the same set in test/, the root and releases/<version>/): index.html is the page and a small loader; style.css is the look of the menus and buttons; game.js is the game code (three.js r170 from jsDelivr through an import map); game-data.json is all the content. No build step.
- The loader fetches game-data.json fresh, then loads style.css and game.js with its version and build added to their addresses, so phones never mix old and new files.
- game-data.json: all content. Items, recipes, needs (stats), things to use (sources), day and night colours, character looks, body shapes, settings. It has a "help" section explaining each part.
- Adding content should almost always be a small edit to game-data.json, not code.
- The world is settings.worldSize metres square (100 for now), with thick forest past the edge. The clearing round the start is laid out from its own random numbers and the rest of the world from a second set, so changing the world never reshuffles the clearing.
- Keep it light for phones. Lots of copies of a shape are drawn as one InstancedMesh, split into chunks of the map. Grass, flowers and pebbles are only drawn near the camera. Far-off trees use simpler shapes.
- The game saves on the phone (localStorage) every few seconds and when the page is hidden: your person, inventory, needs, where things lie and how far each berry bush has grown back. The test link and the release keep separate saves. Saves are plain data (SAVE_FORMAT in index.html; bump it if the save's shape changes), ready to move to a server when the world goes online.
- server/ is the online world: a Cloudflare Worker (named pocket-life) with one Durable Object per world (test, live), at wss://pocket-life.misty-glade-6e8e.workers.dev/world. Cloudflare builds and deploys it from the server folder on every push to main. The game finds it through "server" in game-data.json, and plays offline if it can't connect.
- If the frame rate drops below about 45, the game lowers its sharpness a step (pixel ratio), never below 1.

## Working on it
- Build in small steps. After each change, tell Luke in a few short lines what changed and what to try on his phone.
- Bump the version with every update (0.0.6, then 0.0.7 and so on). It's only in game-data.json: "version", plus "build" which starts at 1 for each new version and counts up with each test copy. The page shows it, like "v0.0.6 (test 2)".
- To test needs quickly, raise settings.needsSpeed (60 makes an hour pass in a minute). Put it back to 1 before pushing.
- game-data.json is loaded with fetch, so opening index.html straight from the disk won't work. Test with a local server (python3 -m http.server) or the test link.
- Test first, then release. Make every change in the test/ folder, push it to main, and tell Luke to try it on the test link: https://lukeogston97-hash.github.io/pocket-life/test/
- Each new test build: bump "build", then run python3 tools/worlds.py test-build so older test games move to it.
- Maintenance: Luke switches it on and off himself in the game (Admin in the Menu). Release-link players see a Back soon screen, needs pause, and it rejoins by itself. The test link still gets in, so Luke can try the new build first.
- server/worlds.json holds the minVersion for each link. Older games get sent to the newest version (they wait and retry if the link hasn't updated yet).
- Admin: all in the game on Luke's phone (the admin player), not a separate page.
- Only when Luke says to release it, run sh tools/release.sh (it also sets the release link's minVersion; Luke turns maintenance off in the game), then commit and push. It copies test/ to the root (the release link: https://lukeogston97-hash.github.io/pocket-life/) and keeps a copy in releases/<version>/ so we can roll back.
- Push to main, not only to a separate branch, or neither link updates. GitHub Pages updates about a minute or two after each push.
- No em-dashes in anything Luke reads.
