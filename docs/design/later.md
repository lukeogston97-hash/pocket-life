# Planned and not built yet: sign-in, buildings, later ideas, stone

- Sign-in (decided 21 Nov 2026, not built yet): Google sign-in later, optional, so the same person works on phone and PC; signing in keeps the person you already have. The server's whoFor(m) in index.js is where it plugs in (map 'acct:<google id>' to the phone's who). Luke makes the Google Cloud web client ID when we build it.
  - 25 Nov 2026: Luke picked this as the answer to "Keeping your person" in the audit below ("a sign in system, not code"), and confirmed Google, and optional (guests still play with no sign-in). Then parked it the same afternoon ("finish everything else except for the sign in"): nothing is built. When he picks it up again: small steps, with the three questions below first.
  - Still to settle with Luke before building: where Sign in goes (Settings, the main menu, a note after you make a person); what happens when the Google account already has a person and this phone has a different one (nobody's person should vanish by surprise); what the server keeps (Google's account id only, or the email too, so the admin can help someone find their person).
  - Known limits: Google refuses sign-in inside most apps' built-in browsers (a link opened in Facebook, Instagram or TikTok), so there the game has to say to open it in Safari or Chrome first. Luke makes the client ID himself (Claude never handles his passwords or keys; the client ID isn't a secret, it sits in the page).
  - How it's planned to work (Claude's plan, 25 Nov 2026; nothing built yet, and nothing gets built until Luke has answered the three questions above):
    - The game loads Google's sign-in script only when a player opens Sign in, so a guest's game never talks to Google. The button is Google's own (they draw it; the dark pill is the closest to the game's look). Google hands the game a signed note saying which account it is (an ID token).
    - The game sends that note to the server on the socket it already has. The server never takes the game's word for it: it checks Google's signature with Google's public keys (fetched now and then, and kept), that the note was made for Pocket Life's client ID, and that it hasn't run out (they last an hour). Google's advice is to use only the note's "sub" (the account's number) as the id.
    - The server then links this phone's id to the account, and whoFor(m) gives the account's person for every linked phone. First sign-in: the account takes the person this phone already has. Sign out: the phone goes back to being its own guest id. The phone's id stays the key that proves who's asking, so nothing new is kept on the phone.
    - Storage writes only at sign-in and sign-out, and one fetch of Google's keys every few hours at most: well inside the free plan.
    - Claude's picks for the three questions, if Luke has no strong view: a row in Settings plus a small one-time note after you make a person; the game asks before swapping ("Play as Mia?") and the phone's own person is left behind, not removed (they come back if you sign out, and leave the world after 3 days away as usual); the server keeps Google's account number only, no email or name.
    - Steps, each a small test build: 1 the server's side and the admin's (who's signed in, unlink), with nothing showing in the game; 2 the game's side (Sign in, the swap question, the "open in Safari or Chrome" note) and Privacy and rules; 3 switched on with Luke's client ID, tried on a phone and a computer; 4 bans that follow the account, and the forget-a-player tool.
  - Luke's part, about ten minutes, free (the page names are Google's, read 25 Nov 2026 from developers.google.com/identity/gsi/web/guides/get-google-api-clientid): in Google Cloud Console, under Google Auth Platform, (1) Branding: the app's name (Pocket Life), his support email, and later the privacy page's address (https://pocketlife.pages.dev/privacy.html, live once 0.1.10 is released; it has to be on an authorised domain); (2) Clients: Create client, Web application, and under Authorised JavaScript origins https://pocketlife.pages.dev (no path; no redirect address is needed); (3) send Claude the Client ID (it ends .apps.googleusercontent.com and isn't a secret). Never the client secret. To check when we get there: whether the app has to be published (Audience) before people other than Luke can sign in.
  - Goes with it, in the same updates: Privacy and rules ("There are no accounts yet" stops being true, and Google's consent screen needs the page's address); the admin's side (who is signed in, unlink); a ban that follows the account as well as the phone; and a way for the admin to forget a player completely (their person, past people and feedback), which the page's "have your person removed" leans on. - Fishing and cooking (0.3.4, Luke 23 Nov 2026; no wheat or bread, fish is the first food): the pond has a Fish action (sources.pond.fish in game-data.json). Tap the pond, Fish, needs a fishing spear (20 uses, wears each try). A try takes 8 s and catches a fish about half the time (chance 0.5), costs 3 stamina. The pond holds 10 fish for everyone (the server keeps w.pond { fish, at }, one comes back every 60 minutes, worked out when someone fishes; Admin > World > Regrow everything refills it). The game doesn't know the count: the server replies 'fished' with what happened (caught, Nothing bites, No fish left, Too heavy). Raw fish can't be eaten; a recipe with near campfire and seconds 20 makes Cooked fish (35 hunger, a berry gives 10); recipes with seconds use their things up when they finish. Fish is a new item shape (SHAPES.fish). Away people don't fish. Later, as one "keeping food" update with storage: food going off (raw about a day, cooked about 3 days, dried a couple of weeks) and a drying rack.

What each one is about, biggest first:
- First steps for new players. After the 15 second tip (Double-tap the ground to walk...) nothing says what the five rings are, that berries and the pond keep your person alive, or what to make first. The craft mat's "Try two stones to start" and the empty inventory's line are the only other tips. Ideas: short tips in the same pill, each shown once as the player gets there (walk, pick up, craft, needs, eat and drink); or a How to play page in the Menu; or both.
- Keeping your person. A person belongs to a random id in the browser's own storage (no sign-in yet), so clearing the browser's data, a private tab, a new phone or a different browser means starting again. A link opened inside Facebook, Instagram or TikTok runs in that app's own browser, with its own storage, so the same player opening the game in Safari later is someone new. An iPhone gives a full-screen home screen app its own storage too, which is why manifest.webmanifest says "display": "browser". Ideas: a "move my person" code from the server (short-lived, typed on the other phone), or Google sign-in as planned above (note Google refuses sign-in inside most apps' built-in browsers).
- Room for a crowd. settings.maxPlaying is 20 to stay inside Cloudflare's free plan (100,000 requests and 100,000 storage writes a day; past either, things fail until 9:30 am Darwin). A public post could put most visitors in the line on the main menu. Workers Paid (USD 5 a month minimum, the first 50 million writes a month included) takes the daily limits away; only Luke can switch it. Then raise Player limit in Admin > Settings.
- Enough sticks, stones and tall grass for a crowd. The world holds 45 stones and 60 sticks (startInWorld), topping up one of each every 5 minutes, and 40 clumps of tall grass (1 twine each, back in 1 to 4 hours). A knife, a stone axe and a campfire take about 8 stones, 4 sticks and 4 twine, so the first 5 or 6 players use up the stones and after that only 1 or 2 new players an hour can get going. The top-up time is a live setting; the amounts aren't. Ideas: bigger amounts and a faster top-up, the amounts as live settings, grass that grows back sooner.
- Privacy and rules (done, 0.1.10 test 2: test/privacy.html and Settings > Privacy and rules). The game is 13+ with open chat and keeps names, the last few chat lines (for reports), feedback and the browser type, and had no privacy note or rules page. Still Luke's to do: read the draft and change what he wants; it names no person or business and gives no email (Feedback in the game is the way to ask), which he may want to add.
